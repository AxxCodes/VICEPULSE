fxceless adopts VIctory-NVIDIA as a new release trades FPS for better lighting

Confirmed by VICEPULSE · Dated maintainer benchmark and public release notes

One maintainer reports about 17 FPS in a 25-minute open-world run on an RTX 4070. The latest upstream release warns of lower FPS with the improved visuals.

On November 27, the maintainer of fxceless’s victory-fork said it had ended its previous development line and adopted VIctory-NVIDIA. The maintainer reports averaging about 17 FPS over 25 minutes in the open world at 2560 × 1440 on an RTX 4070 with 32 GB of RAM. The earlier fxceless code averaged about 10 FPS at a lower render size in different scenes. Those runs cannot establish the size of the improvement on the same route.

The README gives the 17 FPS figure as a reported average and leaves frame generation and crash behavior unspecified for that run. The separate RTX 5070 opening-room test still stands at mostly 26–30 rendered FPS. Frame generation inserts extra display frames, which VICEPULSE excludes from its sustained rendered-FPS target.

The rebased fxceless fork enables experimental ray tracing by default. In a separate 1080p test on the RTX 4070, its maintainer measured roughly 10–12.6 FPS as scene complexity varied. Later on November 27, upstream VIctory-NVIDIA released v2026.11.27.8 with improved water lighting enabled by default and fixes for texture and lighting artifacts. The release notes warn of lower FPS and give no new timed benchmark.

VIctory-NVIDIA remains at 90% in the active VICEPULSE Race. The longer run broadens the evidence, but sustained gameplay near 30 rendered FPS still needs repeatable results across heavier scenes. The next evidence review is November 29 at 8 p.m. Martinique time. December 1–7 remains a low-confidence watch window for a qualifying community build. The release date remains unknown.
